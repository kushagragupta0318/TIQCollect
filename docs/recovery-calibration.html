How to read this report

Every claim below carries one of two marks. The distinction matters more than any single number here, because this scorecard has never been tested against money that actually came back.

Measured

Measured — observed in the dry run against real portfolio data. These figures are reproducible: re-run the scorer and they come back the same. They describe what the scorecard does.

Assumed

Assumed — a hand-chosen constant or judgement built into the scorecard. Nothing validates these. They were picked by reasoning, not fitted to outcomes, and they are load-bearing for every number on this page.

There is no third category yet. No prediction here has been compared with a realised recovery, because no labelled outcome exists — the first 30-day figures land 30 days after the first real scoring run.

The operational finding

71 loans — 13.5% of the book — carry half the recoverable money. The top 50 alone account for ₹8.73 Cr of the ₹21.81 Cr. Meanwhile the LOW band is 125 loans, nearly a quarter of the agents' case list, holding 7.2% of the recoverable value.

This concentration is measured from real balances, and it holds regardless of whether the scorecard's absolute rates are well calibrated — it depends on the ordering, not the levels.

Assumed

87% of the mean estimate is BASE_RATE. Of the 40.1% mean 90-day rate, 35.0 points are the starting constant and only +4.5 points net are contributed by everything the scorecard reads about a loan. The factors are individually large — they move a loan by ±24 and ∓20 points on average — but across the book they very nearly cancel.

Read carefully, that is two separate statements. The factors are doing real work ranking loans against each other: 308 loans finish net positive and 217 net negative, and the spread from 2.0% to 82.1% is entirely factor-driven. What the factors are not doing is setting the portfolio's overall level. That comes from a constant chosen by reasoning, never fitted to a recovery.

So the ordering and the level deserve different amounts of trust. The ordering rests on measured loan attributes. The level rests on one number nobody has tested.

What would settle it

The recovered_amount_30/60/90 columns added by this work are exactly the evidence needed. Once the first cohort matures, the mean realised recovery can be compared with the 40.7% estimate directly. Until then no recommendation about BASE_RATE can be grounded in this data, and none is made here.

Note on the arithmetic: the mean of the raw sums is 39.5%, and the reported 40.1% is slightly higher because 24 loans were clamped up to the 2.0% floor. Nothing hit the 95% ceiling.

Collateral is close to deciding the label on its own

SECURITY is the single largest contributor on 424 of 525 loans (81%), against a nominal share of 17.8% — it realises 1.8× its designed influence. The mechanism is visible in the data: it is the only binary factor. Every other factor scales with how late, how much, how recently; SECURITY fires at its full ±0.16 or abstains, so it never lands anywhere in the middle.

The consequence shows in the band composition. Of 165 HIGH loans, 139 are secured, 25 are BUSINESS loans where the factor abstained, and exactly 1 is unsecured. Median 90-day rate is 60.1% for secured against 29.1% for unsecured — a 31-point gap, close to the full 32-point swing the factor can produce by itself.

This may well be correct: collateral genuinely drives recovery, and a HIGH band that means "secured" is not obviously wrong. But it is a business judgement, not a technical one — it decides whether the label tells a manager something they could not get from the loan type column alone. No weight change is proposed here, because nothing in this data shows the current weighting produces a worse ordering than an alternative. Realised recoveries split by secured/unsecured would settle it.

Two behaviours worth a business opinion

Needs a decision

Both surfaced from the real data rather than from reading the code, and both are matters of what the business thinks these signals mean — not arithmetic errors.

Positive payment momentum with no money received

227 loans (43%) scored a positive PAYMENT_MOMENTUM contribution while nothing at all arrived in the behaviour window. 77 of those are 90+ DPD. The factor credits the size of the last recorded payment relative to an EMI even when that payment is long past, so a loan whose last payment was 223 days ago still earns credit for it having been a decent-sized payment.

The reason lines read oddly as a result — one LOW case shows "₹0 received recently; last payment covered 64% of an EMI" attached to a positive score. Whether historic payment size should count as momentum when nothing is currently moving is a collections judgement.

Arrears share reads as positive on deeply distressed loans

123 loans at 90+ DPD received a positive ARREARS_SHARE contribution. The factor rewards a low ratio of overdue amount to total balance. On a long-tenor loan that ratio is low because the balance is large, not because the borrower is nearly current — a 210-DPD NPA with 19% of a ₹15.9 lakh balance overdue scores as favourably on this factor as a genuinely mildly-delinquent account.

Neither is a scoring bug in the sense of producing a wrong number from its own rules; both are cases where the rule may not mean what its name suggests to a collections manager reading it.

Coverage is high because the always-available factors — ageing, arrears share, penal drag, payment recency — speak on essentially every loan. The two frequent abstentions are LEGAL_POSTURE (83.4% of loans) and NPA_STATUS (67.6%), both of which only carry information once an account is deep enough for the bank to have acted.

An assumption worth naming

Recovery has no configured coverage floor. The repayment scorecard suppresses a number below 55% coverage; recovery shows a band regardless. On this book that affects 2 loans, so it is not currently material — but it is a deliberate difference between the two scores that nothing has validated.

Where that leaves the decision

The ordering is measured and defensible: it separates ₹10.62 Cr of recoverable value from ₹1.58 Cr using real loan attributes, and it concentrates half the money into 13.5% of the book. Nothing in this dry run contradicts it.

The levels are not. Any statement of the form "we will recover ₹21.81 Cr" inherits an untested constant for 87% of its value, and should not be quoted to anyone outside this review as a forecast.

Two items need a business opinion before the label drives field work: whether a HIGH band that is 139/165 secured is telling managers something they did not already know, and whether historic payment size should count as current momentum. Neither is answerable from this data alone.
