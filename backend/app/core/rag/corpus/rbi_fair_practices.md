# Fair-practice conduct in recovery — plain-language summary

This is a plain-language summary of widely known fair-practice principles
for loan recovery in India (the kind of conduct rule a regulated lender's
own Fair Practices Code embodies), written for an LLM as STYLE and TONE
guidance. It is NOT a legal document, NOT a substitute for the bank's own
compliance policy, and it grounds no figure — a generated narrative must
never cite this file as a source of fact, only of tone.

## What the conduct rules generally require

- **Civil, non-coercive contact.** A recovery agent or communication must
  not intimidate, use abusive language, or make a threat of harm. Pressure
  applied through repeated, excessive contact is itself treated as
  harassment, regardless of how polite any one contact is in isolation.
- **Respect for contact hours.** Contact with a borrower for recovery
  purposes is expected to stay within reasonable hours of the day, not at
  times that amount to harassment by timing alone.
- **Right information, right person.** Communication about a debt should
  go to the borrower (or a nominated contact), not be used to shame the
  borrower to third parties, neighbours, or an employer.
- **Clarity about the debt.** The borrower is entitled to know what is
  owed, why, and what the consequence of continued non-payment is, stated
  as fact rather than as a threat.
- **A path to resolve a complaint.** A borrower who believes they have been
  treated unfairly is entitled to a grievance channel, and that channel
  must actually be reachable.
- **Proportionality.** The response to delinquency should be proportionate
  to how it has actually progressed — a first missed instalment does not
  warrant the same approach as a loan already in legal proceedings.

## What this means for AI-generated guidance in this product

Any agent-facing text this product generates (a visit strategy, a
performance insight) must read as the ABOVE kind of conduct at every
delinquency stage: factual, civil, and specific about the debt and the
next step, never coercive or threatening in its phrasing, and never
suggesting contact outside reasonable hours or contact with anyone other
than the borrower and their nominated contact. A draft that reads as a
threat, an ultimatum backed by implied harm, or pressure through repetition
should be rewritten in a factual register before it reaches a field agent.
