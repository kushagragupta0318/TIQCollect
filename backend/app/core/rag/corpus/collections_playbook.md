# Collections playbook

Reference guidance on tone and approach for field collections, organised by
how far a case has progressed. This informs STYLE and GUIDANCE in an
AI-written note — it names no bank, no borrower, and no figure, and nothing
here is a source of fact for a narrative. Every number in a generated note
still comes from the case data passed alongside this file.

## Soft-call stage (early delinquency, typically CURRENT to BUCKET_1)

The borrower has usually missed one instalment, often for an ordinary
reason — a delayed salary, a forgotten standing instruction, a short-term
cash crunch. The right tone is a reminder, not a warning: state the amount
and due date plainly, offer the easiest payment channel, and close with a
specific next step ("pay by Friday" beats "pay soon"). A soft-call PTP that
slips once is routine and should not be escalated in tone; a second slip on
the same promise is the point to move to a field visit.

## Field stage (BUCKET_1 to BUCKET_2)

A field visit earns its cost by doing three things a call cannot: confirming
the borrower still lives or works at the recorded address, reading the
household's actual situation (a closed shop, a family medical event, a
genuine refusal to engage), and collecting evidence (a photo, a signature,
a new promise with a specific date and amount). An agent's note should
distinguish "cannot pay yet" from "will not engage" — the first is a
rescheduling conversation, the second is an escalation signal for the next
stage, not a reason to visit again immediately with the same approach.

## Pre-legal and legal stage (BUCKET_3 to NPA)

By this stage the borrower has had several genuine opportunities to engage.
The approach shifts from reminder to consequence, but the TONE must not:
a notice of pending legal or SARFAESI action is a statement of fact about
the loan's status, not a threat delivered in person, and the field agent's
own conduct stays identical to earlier stages — civil, factual, never
raising voice or implying harm. Settlement offers at this stage should be
framed around what the borrower can realistically pay now, not around
pressure.

## NPA recovery

By NPA, the loan has usually been delinquent for three months or more and
the borrower's capacity to pay in full is often genuinely gone. A
one-time-settlement conversation should lead with the discount being
offered and the finality of the arrangement, phrased as an opportunity the
borrower is choosing, not an ultimatum. Recording the borrower's own stated
reason for non-payment (job loss, business failure, dispute over the loan
itself) matters more at this stage than at any earlier one — it is what the
next stage's strategy is actually built on.
